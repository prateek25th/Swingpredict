About the Models

Each model has its own entry logic, but they all share the same ATR-scaled target/stop framework and the same 1-2 week (5-10 bar) horizon.

Why these four?

Across both Indian and global swing-trading research, the same short list of indicators keeps coming up as the most reliable for a 1-2 week horizon: 50-EMA (trend), RSI(14) (momentum exhaustion), MACD(12,26,9) (momentum direction), Bollinger Bands(20,2) (volatility), Donchian-20 (range), ATR(14) (sizing), and 20-day average volume (participation).

Rather than pick a single 'best' combination, we run four independent models that each combine these primitives differently. The dashboard publishes the historical hit-rate per model per stock so you can see empirically which one works best on each name — rather than trusting any one approach in the abstract.

Trend-Momentum Pullback

Buy healthy pullbacks inside an established uptrend.

Markets in real uptrends don't move in straight lines — they make two steps forward, one step back. The best risk:reward entries come on the 'one step back' phase, after the herd has already pushed momentum indicators into oversold territory but the larger trend is still intact. This is the most-cited bread-and-butter swing setup for Indian equities.

Entry rules

  • Close is above the 50-day EMA (price is in a structural uptrend).
  • The 50-EMA today is higher than it was 5 sessions ago (trend slope is up).
  • RSI(14) is between 40 and 55 — pulled back from overbought but not crashing.
  • MACD histogram has turned up for two consecutive bars (momentum rotating up).
  • Today's volume is at or above the 20-day average (real participation).

Target / Stop

Target = trigger + 3.0 x ATR(14). Stop = trigger - 1.5 x ATR(14). Reward:Risk = 2.0.

Breakout + Volume

Catch fresh expansion out of a coiled, low-volatility range.

Bollinger Band 'squeezes' — periods where volatility contracts to an unusual low — reliably precede explosive moves. The two failure modes for naive breakout systems are low-volume fakeouts and breakouts that fight the larger trend; this model filters out both.

Entry rules

  • Close breaks above the prior 20-day Donchian high (a fresh range expansion).
  • Volume on the breakout is at least 1.5x the 20-day average.
  • Bollinger bandwidth was in the bottom 10% of the last 100 sessions within the prior 10 bars — confirming a real squeeze preceded the move.
  • Close is still above the 50-day EMA (breakout aligns with the bigger trend).

Target / Stop

Target = trigger + 3.0 x ATR(14). Stop = trigger - 1.5 x ATR(14). Reward:Risk = 2.0.

Mean-Reversion (in-trend)

Buy statistical overshoots, but only inside an uptrend.

Plain RSI < 30 on Indian equities often signals real damage, not a buyable dip. The fix is to add a regime filter: only take oversold bounces when the larger trend (50-EMA slope) is still rising. The rejection-bar requirement ensures buyers actually stepped in on the day.

Entry rules

  • Today's low touched or pierced the lower Bollinger Band (statistical overshoot).
  • RSI(14) closed below 30 (momentum fully washed out).
  • The 50-day EMA is still rising vs. 5 sessions ago (uptrend regime intact).
  • Today's close is above today's open — a rejection bar showing buyers responded.

Target / Stop

Target = trigger + 4.0 x ATR(14). Stop = trigger - 2.0 x ATR(14) (wider stop because mean-reversion entries need more breathing room). Reward:Risk = 2.0.

Multi-Indicator Confluence

Fire only when 3 of 4 independent indicator families all agree.

Case studies cited across both Indian and global swing-trading literature put the lift at roughly +15 percentage points of win-rate vs. single-indicator systems. This model fires far less often than the others, but historically with the highest hit-rate.

Entry rules

  • Trend vote: close > 50-EMA AND 50-EMA rising.
  • Momentum vote: MACD line > signal line AND RSI > 50.
  • Volatility vote: close is above the Bollinger midline but below the upper band (room to run).
  • Volume vote: today's volume ≥ 1.2x the 20-day average.
  • At least 3 of these 4 votes must be True, and the vote count must have just crossed up to 3 (prevents firing every bar inside an existing trend).

Target / Stop

Target = trigger + 3.5 x ATR(14). Stop = trigger - 1.5 x ATR(14). Reward:Risk = 2.3.

Shared mechanics

Trigger / Target / Stop

  • Trigger = the close of the signal bar.
  • Stop = trigger − k_stop × ATR(14). ATR-scaling means stops adapt to each stock's own volatility.
  • Target = trigger + k_target × ATR(14). Sized so reward:risk ≥ 2:1 on every model.
  • Time stop = exit at the close of the 10th session if neither target nor stop has been hit.

Historical hit-rate (the published probability)

For every fresh signal we replay the same model rules across the prior 5 years of that stock's history, walk every past signal forward up to 10 bars, and report the empirical fraction that hit target before stop. That is the number shown as 'Hit-rate' on the dashboard. It uses only bars after each historical signal — no look-ahead.

Confidence label

  • High: hit-rate ≥ 65% on a sample of at least 8 historical signals.
  • Medium: hit-rate between 50% and 64%.
  • Low: hit-rate below 50%.
  • Unknown: fewer than 8 historical signals to compute a reliable hit-rate.
